The napkin proof

  1. 1/3 = 0.333…you already believe this
  2. × 3both sides
  3. 1 = 0.999…done

Still suspicious? Name a number between 0.999… and 1. You can't. Two different numbers always have room between them. These two don't.

The same idea kills a 2,400-year-old paradox about walking across a room→

Maths · Explainer

Zeno says you can never cross a room

First you walk half. Then half of what's left. Then half again. That's infinitely many steps, so you never arrive. Right?

1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + … = 1

Infinitely many steps, finite distance. You get to the door. Exactly the way 0.999… gets to 1.

Adding infinitely many tiny pieces is the whole trick behind calculus. Swipe up↑

Physics · Predict

Apollo 15, 1971. An astronaut drops a hammer and a feather on the Moon.

Which hits the ground first?

Bet XP

No air, so nothing slows the feather. Gravity pulls every mass at the same rate: 1.62 m/s² up there. From about 1.6 m, that's 1.4 seconds, side by side.
